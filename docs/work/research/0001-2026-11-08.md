# Google Play trader status

Not legal or tax advice. Google says to ask a legal professional, and the tax points need a Turkish accountant (mali müşavir).

## The answer

- It is a yes-or-no statement to Google: do you run your apps as a business, in the sense of EU and UK consumer law?
- Having no company does not settle it. The definition covers any "natural or legal person" acting for their trade, business, craft or profession.
- Every account must answer by 1 February 2027, wherever its apps are offered. That was 85 days from this report.
- The cost of "trader" on a personal account: phone and address public to users everywhere from 1 March 2027.
- An individual earning from apps offered in the EEA or UK is, on Google's own wording, very likely a trader. Nothing found gives a small-earner exception.

## What Google's pages say

Read in full on 2026-11-08: [trader status](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17594752?hl=en), [account information](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13628312?hl=en), [account type](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13634885?hl=en), [country rules](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6223646?hl=en).

## Conflicts and gaps

- **Monetizing may already show the address.** The account page says that if a personal account monetizes, "Google will display your full address". It draws no trader distinction, and no page says whether that changes. So non-trader may not hide the address of an earning account.
- **Address source.** Taken from the Google Payments profile. No Google page offers a separate display address, and "virtual office" appears nowhere. Apple does allow an alternate address with documents. Whether a business address works on a personal Google account is unknown.
- **Phone.** Personal traders must give a contact number for display. No page says whether it may differ from the private account number.
- **Geo-blocking.** The country page asks for no "unjustified geo-blocking" in the EU. It does not say whether leaving the whole EU counts, while the trader page plainly allows the non-distribution route.
- **Legal basis.** Google cites the EU Unfair Commercial Practices Directive and the UK DMCC Act. Third-party guides that say "DSA" look to be mixing this up with Apple's rule.

## EU law behind it

- [Commission guidance, section 2.2](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:52021XC1229(05)): trader has a "particularly broad meaning", decided case by case, no income figure. It does not mention ad revenue; that example is Google's. The guidance has no legal force of its own.
- [Kamenova, C-105/17](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62017CJ0105): 8 non-exhaustive signs, including organised activity, profit aim, expertise, legal status, VAT and pay. A profit aim alone "is not sufficient". It is about goods, not apps, and no source applies it to apps.
- So a very small earner has an honest argument in law, but it is untested, and declaring on that argument carries Google's removal risk.

## Türkiye

- Google's Turkish page says "tacir", the Turkish word for merchant. It cites EEA and UK law, not Turkish law, and warns the page may be machine translated. Not being a tacir under Turkish commercial law does not answer Google's question.
- [318 Tebliğ, current text](https://www.mevzuat.gov.tr/anasayfa/MevzuatFihristDetayIframe?MevzuatTur=9&MevzuatNo=39273&MevzuatTertip=5), article 6(2): these earnings are "taxed as commercial income", then exempted. That gives no support to calling the activity non-commercial. This step is this report's own reading; no source connects 20/B to Google's status.
- Also from it: individuals only, corporate taxpayers excluded; exemption certificate; revenue collected only through a dedicated account at a bank in Türkiye; 15% withheld.
- Ad income is listed, but only through app store platforms. AdMob is not named. Ask the accountant.
- The 2026 limit, 5,300,000 TL, comes from secondary search results only. The official text's latest figure is 3,000,000 TL for 2024.
- A limited company ends 20/B. One unverified source says a sole proprietorship can keep it.

## The options

| Option | Allowed? | Gain | Cost | Undo |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trader, personal, every country | Yes | All users, fully honest | Home address and phone public worldwide | Address cannot be unpublished once copied |
| Remove EEA and UK, non-trader | Yes, stated by Google | Address stays off this route, unless monetizing shows it anyway | Those users and their earnings | Add countries back and change status later |
| Non-trader while earning in EEA or UK | No, unless truly non-commercial | Nothing lasting | Global removal risk | Hard after suspension |
| Company, organization account | Yes | Company address shows, not home | Ends 20/B if a limited company; costs; D-U-N-S up to 30 days; cannot go back to personal | Hard |
| Business address on personal account | Unknown | Home stays private | Unverified; must be a true legal address | Unknown |

## Recommendation

1. Declare nothing today. There is time, and one fact is missing.
2. Check the public listing of each Play app: does it show the address now, and does it show ads or purchases?
3. Check Play Console statistics: how much of the earnings and users come from the EEA and UK?
4. Default: remove EEA and UK from every app, then declare non-trader. It is the only allowed option that can be fully undone.
5. Switch to trader only if those regions bring real money, and ask the accountant about a lawful non-home address before 1 March 2027.
6. If the listing already shows the address, the privacy gain is gone, so declare trader and keep every country.
7. Finish by mid-January 2027, ahead of the 1 February deadline.
