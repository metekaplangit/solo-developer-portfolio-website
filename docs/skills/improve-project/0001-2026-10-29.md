## What the Safari measurement settled

All earlier line-break measurements (text-project 0001-0003, issue 0007) used headless Chrome. The site's visitors buy Mac and iPhone apps, so many browse in Safari. And WebKit's `pretty` and `balance` work on the whole paragraph, where Chrome's work on the last 4 lines only (WebKit blog, 2025-04-08). So this run measured both engines.

`scripts/probe/wrap-engines.mjs`, 8 routes, built site from commit 0fed351, Playwright WebKit build 2370 and system Chrome:

| Width | Wrap | Engine | Paragraphs | Lines | Bad line ends | Average fill |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1440 | balance | Chromium | 66 | 149 | 3 | 0.72 |
| 1440 | balance | WebKit | 78 | 182 | 3 | 0.71 |
| 1440 | pretty | Chromium | 10 | 24 | 2 | 0.91 |
| 1440 | pretty | WebKit | 10 | 24 | 2 | 0.88 |
| 390 | balance | Chromium | 97 | 301 | 4 | 0.77 |
| 390 | balance | WebKit | 102 | 319 | 4 | 0.77 |
| 390 | pretty | Chromium | 15 | 55 | 2 | 0.89 |
| 390 | pretty | WebKit | 15 | 53 | 2 | 0.85 |

- Bad line ends on the rule's core words are the same in both engines. Safari visitors see what the Chrome checks measure.
- WebKit wraps a few more paragraphs onto 2 or more lines (78 against 66 at 1440). This is font metrics, not a fault.
- Load: other sessions were running on the Mac. Both engines ran in one pass.

## Below the bar, recorded

- Issue 0007 (and 0015): its finding says the remaining 31 breaks "need their words changed; no CSS or tie rule mends them". This run found one method that may mend some of them without overflowing at 320 px. Bind pairs longer than 22 characters inside a span that is `white-space: nowrap` only above a width breakpoint (a media query), and keep no-break spaces for the short pairs. Most of 0007's entries break at 1920 and 1440 only. Not worked here, because filed issues wait for the day the owner names. This line is for that day.
