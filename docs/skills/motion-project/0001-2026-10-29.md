# Motion 0001

Lean: none typed — the whole site.
Platform: `PRODUCT.md` records web; the root `CLAUDE.md` carries no platform line. Worked for desktop Chrome/Safari at 1440 px.
Design language: no `docs/DESIGN-LANGUAGE.md`; `DESIGN.md` (Spectrum) is the language. It names motion only as "settles rather than bounces, always reduced-motion safe"; the real values live in `src/styles/global.css` and win.

## 1. The product and how it moves today

- A one-person studio's static Astro 7 site: 3 products, privacy, support, about. Visitors arrive on a Mac from a link and judge craft and trust.
- All motion is CSS, plus 1 small gallery script. No motion library. Tokens: `--dur-fast` 120 ms, `--dur` 220 ms, `--dur-slow` 420 ms; `--ease` cubic-bezier(.22,1,.36,1), `--ease-out` cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1).
- Constraints: static only, no runtime beyond progressive enhancement; reduced motion turns every animation and view transition off (DESIGN §6, deliberate); a scroll-driven header "settle" was tried and removed on 2026-08-01 because it could not be observed in the harness.
- Firefox (late 2026) has neither cross-document view transitions nor `animation-timeline: view()`: Firefox visitors get hard page cuts and no reveals. Content never depends on either.
- Already right, keep: the home hero entrance (opacity + 12 px rise, 60 ms stagger, settles in about 630 ms, 0 dropped frames); hover lifts (1–3 px, 215–232 ms); reveals that move content but never hide it; header and footer held still during page changes; the reduced-motion switch.
- Not a copy of another product; nothing is protected for that reason.

## 7. Legal caution

Every idea is `common-pattern`. COMP-01 is the one closest to a single rival (Rogue Amoeba's travelling icons); reshaped here as this site's own: the icon leaves a full-bleed coloured band carrying a fading glow of its product's hue and lands in a dark editorial header, on this site's 420 ms curve. Design judgement, not a clearance.

## 8. Themes and conflicts

- 3 ideas (USER-01, COMP-01, AI-01) are parts of one change: page changes stop being whole-page fades and become named parts moving. They are separate picks, but build in this order: USER-01 first, then the named parts on top.
- USER-01 and USER-02 pull opposite ways on the page opening vs text reveals; they do not conflict (one is load, one is scroll).
- COMP-05 replaces the dot's `width` animation; issue 0014 (faint inactive dots) sits in the same element and is separate.
- AI-02 conflicts with the architecture's no-runtime guardrail; listed, not picked.
- COMP-07 conflicts with DESIGN §6; listed, not picked.

## 10. The 5 picked

Bar: a visitor would feel it. All 5 clear it. Fallback: not needed. No celebration: family 7 does not apply.

1. **USER-01 Every page lands the way the home page does** — feel it: every page change and every arrival from a link (how this audience arrives) now either overlaps two pages or shows nothing; the hero's arrival, already the site's best moment, becomes the whole site's. Made ours: rivals either cut or fade whole pages; this reuses the site's own hero rise and 60 ms stagger, with the header holding still.
2. **COMP-01 The product's icon travels with you** — feel it: the one moment a visitor commits to a product carries them visibly from the shelf to its page. Made ours: Rogue Amoeba moves icons between light grids; here it leaves a coloured band with its product's hue glow and lands in a dark header. Shared core: none.
3. **AI-01 The current-page pill glides to where you went** — feel it: the header is on every page and the pill is the only thing in it that changes; it stops blinking. Made ours: no mapped site moves its nav indicator across page loads; it uses the site's existing pill and `--ease`.
4. **COMP-05 The gallery's marker rides with the slide** — feel it: the gallery is each product page's largest element; today the number and dot react a fifth of a second late and separately (measured). Made ours: Apple and Stripe use the technique for state; this ties this site's white pill and counter chip to the slide, and drops the layout-costly `width`.
5. **COMP-06 The screenshot leans in, the frame stays put** — feel it: every Mac visitor points at the screenshots; the frame jumping 3 px becomes the picture coming closer. Made ours: borrowed from a museum, not a rival; slower than the site's UI responses on purpose (600 ms), with the hairline brightening as its only other cue.

Serious, outside the 5: issue 0016 (button press feedback never shows) — filed, not proposed. Firefox visitors get none of ideas 1–4 and keep today's hard cuts.
