# Say nothing about a product's future business model

Produced by: plan-task-cards, the owner typed the command.
Date: 2026-09-10.
Covers: removing every "no ads / no tracking / no subscription / offline forever"
promise from the site's marketing copy, built against commit `ec49d50`. Written
to `docs/plans/` — the project's own plan home, named in `control/README.md`.
`docs/skills/plan-task-cards/` does not exist here and was not created.

## What this is

The site currently promises, in 17 places, that products carry no advertising,
no in-app purchases, no subscription, no tracking and no networking. Every one
of those was true when it was written. None of them is a fact about the product
— they are commitments about what the owner will never do, and the owner intends
to keep the option to add advertising, in-app purchases and network features to
future releases.

The FTC treats an objective claim of this kind as one that must be truthful and
substantiated; making it and then reversing it after acquiring users is the
textbook deceptive-advertising pattern. So the claims come off the marketing
copy entirely, rather than being softened or hedged.

The privacy pages are deliberately untouched. Apple requires a privacy policy
that describes what an app does today, and App Store Connect disclosures must be
kept current whenever practices change. Those documents are supposed to be
version-specific statements of current fact, and they get revised when a release
revises them. Stripping them would leave the products with no privacy policy at
all, which is a worse position than the one this plan is fixing.

What replaces them is the set of facts that stay true whatever the business
model becomes: the price today, the platform, the system requirement, and that a
real person answers support. Where a privacy stance was being asserted, the
privacy page is the thing to point at — it is the document that carries a date
and gets revised.

The facts line is the one place a visitor will notice the change: it drops from
three facts to two. That is a plan-level note rather than a card-level decision,
because the owner sees it.

Left to the card: whether `privacyFacts` survives as a schema field with new
content or leaves the schema entirely, and the exact replacement wording.

- **Prior art:** FTC advertising guidance — objective claims must be truthful and
  substantiated (ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/advertising-faqs-guide-small-business,
  read 2026-09-10). Apple App Privacy Details — disclosures must be kept accurate
  and current in App Store Connect whenever practices change
  (developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/, read 2026-09-10).
- **Avoid:** hedging the claims ("currently free of ads") rather than removing
  them. A dated qualifier is still an objective claim about the product and
  still has to be maintained; it also reads as a warning that ads are coming.
- **Constraint:** the privacy pages state current fact and stay. Nothing in this
  card touches `src/content/policies/` or `src/components/PolicyArticle.astro`.
- **Prior art in the owner's other projects:** none reusable. Searched every
  project beside this one for `advertis subscription in-app privacyFacts
  monetiz offline tracking claim`; the hits are the apps' own copy and release
  reports, not a prior removal of claims.
